## Introduction

Dalhousie Castle’s Grey Lady is [Midlothian]({{ 'where-midlothians-ghost-stories-gather/' | relative_url }})’s most polished hotel ghost story: a young woman called Lady Catherine, said to have died after being shut away in a castle turret, is now linked with reported apparitions on stairs, corridors, turrets, bedrooms and dungeon spaces. The tale matters because it is not simply a loose internet rumour. Dalhousie Castle’s own visitor material preserves the tradition, while travel and media retellings show how the story has split into rival versions: in one, Catherine is the mistress of a Ramsay laird punished by his jealous wife; in another, she is a 16-year-old noblewoman separated from a stable hand. The evidence is folkloric rather than documentary proof of a haunting, but the legend is strongly attached to the castle’s Ramsay history, its turreted architecture, and its present life as a working Midlothian castle hotel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

## The Ramsay Castle Setting

Dalhousie Castle stands near Bonnyrigg in Midlothian, close enough to Edinburgh to attract visitors, but set in the older estate-and-river landscape where castle legends tend to thrive. The building’s physical character matters to the Grey Lady story. Historic Environment Scotland describes Dalhousie as a category A listed castle with a mid-15th-century L-plan keep and curtain wall incorporated into later buildings, with 17th-century work, 18th-century alterations and 19th-century castellations. In plainer terms, this is not a single untouched medieval shell, but a layered castle: old core, later remodelling, turrets, stairs, basement spaces and romantic baronial additions all folded into one visitor experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CLB784" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.historicenvironment.scot">[portal.historicenvironment.scot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.historicenvironment.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DALHOUSI E CASTLE (LB784</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DALHOUSI E CASTLE (LB784</span></span></span>

That layered architecture gives the legend a believable stage without proving the events behind it. The hotel’s own material emphasises its long Ramsay association and places the origin of the Grey Lady tradition around 1500, after the addition of the drum tower and well to the existing L-shaped inner keep. In that timeline, the apparition is identified as Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of the Ramsay lairds, locked in a castle turret where she died; her ghost is then said to have been seen on the stairs and in the dungeons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

Dalhousie’s modern hotel identity also shapes the ghost story. The official hotel site presents the castle as a place of “characterful” bedrooms reached by a grand staircase and secret passages, which is almost the perfect built environment for a resident apparition legend. A story set in a ruin is visited at a distance; a story set in a hotel is slept inside. Guests encounter corridors, stairwells, old stonework, uneven histories and night-time sounds as part of the same stay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dalhousiecastle.co.uk.</span></span></span>

For Midlothian’s haunted geography, that makes Dalhousie distinct. [Rosslyn]({{ 'rosslyn/' | relative_url }}) Chapel and Castle lean towards sacred mystery, carved symbolism and glen folklore; Borthwick has royal and castle traditions; Dalhousie’s Grey Lady is more intimate. It is a domestic haunting: jealousy, confinement, a young woman’s death, and a figure still glimpsed in the private routes of a house that has become a hotel.

## Lady Catherine and Rival Versions

The most important thing to know about the Grey Lady is that there is not one stable version of her story. The official Dalhousie version and the hotel’s A to Z page identify her as Lady Catherine, a mistress of a Ramsay laird, locked in a turret by his vengeful wife around 1500 and left to perish. This version ties the haunting to household power, marital jealousy and the vulnerability of a young woman inside an aristocratic castle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

A widely repeated alternative makes Catherine a 16-year-old girl in 1695 who falls in love with a stable hand. In that telling, the scandal is not adultery with a laird but forbidden romance across class boundaries. The Daily Record’s 2013 version describes “Lady Catherine, 16” dying after being banished to the tower in 1695 after being caught with a stable hand, while Celtic Castles’ broader hotel-haunting article repeats the same 1695 stable-hand outline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dailyrecord.co.uk/incoming/heartbroken-lady-catherine-haunts-dalhousie-1744443" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dailyrecord.co.uk">[Daily Record]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dailyrecord.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">heartbroken lady catherine haunts dalhousie 1744443</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">heartbroken lady catherine haunts dalhousie 1744443</span></span></span>

A third variation blends the emotional shape of the stable-hand story with a family-punishment motif: Catherine is sometimes described as a Ramsay daughter or young noblewoman whose family disapproves of a servant romance. In those versions, she may be shut away by her father or family rather than by a jealous wife. This shift is significant because it changes what the legend is “about”. The mistress version is a tale of sexual rivalry and punishment inside a laird’s household; the stable-hand version is a tragic romance about class, youth and parental control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://adventuresintime1775.wixsite.com/adventures-in-time/single-post/2016/08/04/the-history-of-dalhousie-castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: adventuresintime1775.wixsite.com">[Adventures in Time 1775]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">adventuresintime1775.wixsite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the history of dalhousie castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the history of dalhousie castle</span></span></span>

The changing details do not make the story worthless. They make it recognisably folkloric. Castle ghost legends often preserve a strong emotional pattern while allowing names, dates and motives to move around. At Dalhousie, the fixed elements are more stable than the biography: a young woman called Catherine; a forbidden relationship; confinement in a tower or turret; death by starvation, heartbreak or neglect; and a grey-clad apparition tied to stairs, corridors, turrets and lower rooms. The uncertainty sits exactly where a historian would want more evidence: which Catherine, what date, who ordered the confinement, and whether the death appears in estate, parish or legal records.

## Why the Grey Lady Story Stuck

The Grey Lady has lasted because the story joins three powerful ingredients: a plausible castle setting, a simple injustice, and repeated visitor-facing retellings. The official castle timeline gives the tale a Ramsay-family setting and a circa-1500 origin, while later tourism and newspaper accounts provide the more melodramatic 1695 stable-hand version. Neither form reads like a contemporary legal record; both read like inherited tradition adapted for guests, guides and readers who want the castle’s history to have a human face.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

The name “Grey Lady” also helps. Across Britain, “Grey Lady”, “White Lady”, “Green Lady” and similar colour-name apparitions are common in castle and great-house folklore. The title is memorable, visual and flexible: it can describe a dress, a half-seen figure, a class of female apparition, or simply the mood of an old building. Celtic Castles explains Dalhousie’s name in that direct way: her ghost is called the Grey Lady because of her dress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.celticcastles.com/articles/dalhousie-castle-ghost-story/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: celticcastles.com">[Celtic Castles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">celticcastles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Celtic Castles Dalhousie Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Celtic Castles Dalhousie Castle</span></span></span>

The locations attached to the apparition are equally important. Dalhousie’s own Ramsay-family history says she has been seen on stairs and in the dungeons, while other retellings add halls, turrets, corridors, bedrooms and even the end of guests’ beds. These are not random places. They are threshold spaces: stairs between floors, corridors between rooms, turrets above ordinary domestic life, dungeons below it, bedrooms where visitors are alone with the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[dalhousiecastle.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

The story also benefits from Dalhousie being a hotel rather than only a heritage attraction. A guest can read the legend, walk the stairs, eat in the dungeon restaurant or sleep in an upper room, then interpret ordinary castle sensations through the folklore: a tap, a draught, a noise in a corridor, a shadow at a window. That does not prove a ghost; it explains why the legend remains vivid.

## Hotel Lore, Sightings and What Is Actually Claimed

Modern accounts usually describe the Grey Lady as active rather than merely historical. The hotel’s own A to Z page uses careful wording, saying Dalhousie is “alleged” to be haunted and that there have been “reported apparitions” of the Grey Lady. That cautious phrasing is useful: it preserves the tradition without presenting the sightings as verified events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/blog/a-to-z/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[Dalhousie Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a to z</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a to z</span></span></span>

The most repeated claims are visual apparitions and small physical disturbances. Celtic Castles says the Grey Lady has been seen roaming stairs, halls and turrets; it also reports a steward and pipe sergeant account in which the apparition was seen moving on the stairs, with the added detail that the bagpipes failed to play sweetly when she appeared. Other hotel-haunting summaries repeat claims of tapping on doors or shoulders, a woman appearing in rooms, waving from windows, or sitting at the end of a bed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.celticcastles.com/articles/dalhousie-castle-ghost-story/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: celticcastles.com">[Celtic Castles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">celticcastles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Celtic Castles Dalhousie Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Celtic Castles Dalhousie Castle</span></span></span>

These are media reports of a legend and alleged sightings, not independent proof of the apparition. Their value is in showing how Dalhousie’s ghost story has been publicly framed: part romance, part hotel atmosphere, part “haunted Scotland” travel hook.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dailyrecord.co.uk/incoming/heartbroken-lady-catherine-haunts-dalhousie-1744443" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dailyrecord.co.uk">[Daily Record]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dailyrecord.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">heartbroken lady catherine haunts dalhousie 1744443</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">heartbroken lady catherine haunts dalhousie 1744443</span></span></span>

## A Sceptical Reading That Still Respects the Folklore

The weakest part of the Grey Lady tradition is the biographical evidence. The core sources available to ordinary readers do not establish, in a documentary way, that a specific Lady Catherine died by starvation in a Dalhousie turret. Instead, they preserve versions of a story. The official hotel timeline favours the circa-1500 mistress account, while newspaper, travel and hotel-haunting retellings often prefer the 1695 stable-hand romance. The gap between those versions is too large to treat the details as settled fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dalhousiecastle.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/rpc_dalhousiehistoric_web.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dalhousiecastle.co.uk">[dalhousiecastle.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dalhousiecastle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalhousie CastleThe Ramsay FamilyNovember 23, 2023 — Origin of the &#x27;Grey Lady”, an apparition of a Lady Catherine, a mistress of one of t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2023</span></span></span>

There are also good non-paranormal reasons why an old hotel produces ghostly experiences. Dalhousie has thick stonework, old stairs, upper rooms, basements, remodellings and a strong pre-existing legend. In such a setting, ordinary sounds can become meaningful after dark: pipes, doors, footsteps, staff movement, wind, heating systems, neighbouring guests, creaking timber, or the heightened attention that comes after hearing a ghost story. None of these explanations disproves an individual witness’s experience, but they do offer a grounded way to understand why sightings cluster around suggestive places such as stairs, corridors, bedrooms and dungeons.

The folklore reading may be the most useful one. The Grey Lady condenses anxieties that belong naturally to castles: family control, unequal power, hidden rooms, sexual reputation, confinement and the fear that private suffering can be buried inside grand architecture. In the mistress version, Catherine is punished for a relationship that threatens a household. In the stable-hand version, she is punished for crossing class boundaries. In both, the apparition returns through the very spaces that made her powerless: stairs she could not freely descend, turrets she could not leave, rooms where she was seen but not saved.

That is why the story works even when the evidence is uncertain. Dalhousie’s Grey Lady gives Midlothian a haunted-place tradition that is specific, memorable and place-bound. It belongs to the castle’s red stone, Ramsay inheritance, turreted skyline and modern hotel corridors — not as proven supernatural fact, but as one of the clearest examples of how a local legend can turn an old building into an eerie story people still want to enter.
